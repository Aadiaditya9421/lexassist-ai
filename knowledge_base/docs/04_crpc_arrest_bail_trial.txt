=== Code of Criminal Procedure (CrPC) 1973 ===

The Code of Criminal Procedure, 1973, is an exhaustive and comprehensive piece of legislation that outlines the procedures to be followed in criminal cases in India. This document provides a detailed overview of the CrPC, focusing on arrest, bail, and trial procedures.

--- ARREST (S.41-60)

The CrPC provides for the procedures to be followed when making an arrest. The following sections are relevant:

* S.41: 9 conditions for arrest without warrant. The police can arrest a person without a warrant if they satisfy any of the following conditions:
	1. The person has been concerned in a cognizable offence.
	2. The person is suspected to be a deserter from the armed forces.
	3. The person is a proclaimed offender.
	4. The person is suspected to be a person against whom a reasonable complaint has been made.
	5. The person is suspected to be a person who has in his possession any implement of house-breaking.
	6. The person is suspected to be a person who has been concerned in any offence punishable with imprisonment for a term of 4 years or more.
	7. The person is suspected to be a person who has been concerned in any offence punishable with imprisonment for a term of less than 4 years, but the person is likely to commit a further offence.
	8. The person is suspected to be a person who has been concerned in any offence punishable with imprisonment for a term of less than 4 years, but the person is likely to tamper with evidence.
	9. The person is suspected to be a person who has been concerned in any offence punishable with imprisonment for a term of less than 4 years, but the person is likely to intimidate witnesses.
* S.41A: Notice to appear (2009 amendment — Arnesh Kumar guidelines 2014 SC). The police must issue a notice to the person to appear before the police, and the person must be given an opportunity to explain their side of the story.
* S.41B: Procedure of arrest — name, designation of arresting officer. The arresting officer must identify themselves and provide their name and designation to the person being arrested.
* S.41C: Control room at districts. A control room must be established at each district to monitor and oversee the arrest process.
* S.41D: Right to meet advocate during interrogation. The person being arrested has the right to meet with an advocate during interrogation.
* S.46: How arrest is made — no more restraint than necessary; woman arrested only by woman officer after sunset only with magistrate permission. The arrest must be made with minimal restraint, and a woman can only be arrested by a woman officer after sunset with the permission of a magistrate.
* S.49: No unnecessary restraint. The person being arrested must not be subjected to unnecessary restraint.
* S.50: Grounds of arrest to be communicated; right to bail in bailable offence. The person being arrested must be informed of the grounds of arrest and has the right to bail in bailable offences.
* S.50A: Inform nominated person of arrest (2008 amendment). The person being arrested must be allowed to inform a nominated person of their arrest.
* S.54: Medical examination of arrested person. The person being arrested must be provided with a medical examination.
* S.57: Not to be detained beyond 24 hours without magistrate order. The person being arrested must not be detained beyond 24 hours without the order of a magistrate.
* S.167: Remand — judicial custody procedure; default bail (S.167(2)) if chargesheet not filed within 60/90 days (magistrate/sessions triable). The person being arrested must be produced before a magistrate within 24 hours, and if the chargesheet is not filed within 60/90 days, the person is entitled to default bail.

--- BAIL (S.436-450)

The CrPC provides for the procedures to be followed when granting bail. The following sections are relevant:

* S.436: Bailable offences — bail is a right, police/court MUST grant. In bailable offences, bail is a right, and the police or court must grant bail.
* S.436A: Maximum detention — bail after half the maximum sentence period served. If the person has served half of the maximum sentence period, they are entitled to bail.
* S.437: Non-bailable offences — court's discretion; 7 factors; special provisions for women, children under 16, sick/infirm persons. In non-bailable offences, the court has discretion to grant bail, considering the following factors:
	1. The nature and gravity of the offence.
	2. The antecedents of the person.
	3. The possibility of the person fleeing from justice.
	4. The possibility of the person tampering with evidence.
	5. The possibility of the person intimidating witnesses.
	6. The likelihood of the person committing a further offence.
	7. The interest of justice.
* S.437A: Bail on conclusion of trial before higher court. If the person is acquitted or convicted by a lower court, they are entitled to bail pending appeal to a higher court.
* S.438: Anticipatory bail — application to Sessions Court or High Court; conditions court may impose; cannot be time-limited per SC (Sushila Aggarwal 2020). The person can apply for anticipatory bail to the Sessions Court or High Court, and the court may impose conditions.
* S.439: Special powers of HC/Sessions Court — can cancel bail also. The High Court or Sessions Court has special powers to grant or cancel bail.
* S.440-450: Amount of bail, sureties, cash bail, cancellation. The CrPC provides for the procedures to be followed when determining the amount of bail, sureties, cash bail, and cancellation of bail.

--- FIR AND INVESTIGATION (S.154-176)

The CrPC provides for the procedures to be followed when registering a First Information Report (FIR) and conducting an investigation. The following sections are relevant:

* S.154: FIR — must be recorded in writing, read to informant, signed; zero FIR concept; S.154(3) if officer refuses, send written complaint to SP. The FIR must be recorded in writing, read to the informant, and signed.
* S.155: Non-cognizable offences — police need magistrate order. In non-cognizable offences, the police need a magistrate's order to investigate.
* S.157-173: Investigation, spot inspection, inquest (S.174-176 — unnatural death). The police must conduct an investigation, spot inspection, and inquest in cases of unnatural death.
* S.173: Police report (chargesheet) — within 60 days (magistrate triable) or 90 days (sessions). The police must file a chargesheet within 60 days for magistrate triable cases or 90 days for sessions triable cases.

--- TRIAL PROCEDURE

The CrPC provides for the procedures to be followed during a trial. The following sections are relevant:

* S.190-204: Cognizance of offences by magistrate; issue of process. The magistrate must take cognizance of the offence and issue a process.
* S.225-250: Sessions trial — charge (S.228), defence (S.233), judgment (S.235). The sessions trial involves the charge, defence, and judgment.
* S.251-259: Warrant case trial before magistrate. The warrant case trial is conducted before a magistrate.
* S.260-265L: Summary trials; plea bargaining (S.265A-265L, 2005 amendment). The summary trial involves a simplified procedure, and plea bargaining is allowed.
* S.300: Double jeopardy — once acquitted/convicted, cannot be tried for same offence. A person cannot be tried twice for the same offence.
* S.313: Examination of accused — must be given opportunity to explain evidence. The accused must be given an opportunity to explain the evidence against them.
* S.321: Withdrawal from prosecution by public prosecutor. The public prosecutor can withdraw from the prosecution.

### BAIL TYPE COMPARISON TABLE

| Type of Bail | Description | Eligibility |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Regular Bail | Bail granted to a person who is already in custody | Any person who is in custody |
| Anticipatory Bail | Bail granted to a person who has not been arrested yet | Any person who has reason to believe that they will be arrested |
| Interim Bail | Temporary bail granted to a person until the final disposal of the case | Any person who is in custody and has a strong case for bail |
| Default Bail | Bail granted to a person when the police fail to file a chargesheet within the prescribed time limit | Any person who is in custody and the police have failed to file a chargesheet within the prescribed time limit |

### PRACTICAL NOTES

The following are some practical notes for citizens:

1. **Right to Bail**: In bailable offences, bail is a right, and the police or court must grant bail.
2. **Notice to Appear**: The police must issue a notice to the person to appear before the police, and the person must be given an opportunity to explain their side of the story.
3. **Medical Examination**: The person being arrested must be provided with a medical examination.
4. **Informing a Nominated Person**: The person being arrested must be allowed to inform a nominated person of their arrest.
5. **Default Bail**: If the police fail to file a chargesheet within the prescribed time limit, the person is entitled to default bail.
6. **Plea Bargaining**: Plea bargaining is allowed in summary trials, and the accused can negotiate a plea with the prosecution.

### CITIZEN Q&A

1. **Q: What is the difference between a bailable and non-bailable offence?**
A: In a bailable offence, bail is a right, and the police or court must grant bail. In a non-bailable offence, the court has discretion to grant bail.
2. **Q: Can the police arrest a person without a warrant?**
A: Yes, the police can arrest a person without a warrant if they satisfy any of the conditions under S.41 of the CrPC.
3. **Q: What is anticipatory bail?**
A: Anticipatory bail is bail granted to a person who has not been arrested yet, but has reason to believe that they will be arrested.
4. **Q: Can a person be tried twice for the same offence?**
A: No, a person cannot be tried twice for the same offence, as per the principle of double jeopardy.
5. **Q: What is the procedure for filing a FIR?**
A: The FIR must be recorded in writing, read to the informant, and signed.
6. **Q: Can a person apply for bail online?**
A: No, a person cannot apply for bail online. They must apply to the court or police station in person or through a lawyer.
