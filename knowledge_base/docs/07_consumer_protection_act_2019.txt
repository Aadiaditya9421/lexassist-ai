=== CONSUMER PROTECTION ACT, 2019 ===

--- DEFINITIONS (S.2)

The Consumer Protection Act, 2019, defines various terms to ensure clarity and understanding of the provisions. These definitions include:

1. **Consumer (S.2(7))**: A person who buys goods or hires services for consideration, not for resale or commercial purposes. This includes online purchases and beneficiaries of services.
2. **Goods (S.2(21))**: Every kind of moveable property, including food, shares, and growing crops.
3. **Service (S.2(42))**: Services such as banking, financing, insurance, transport, processing, supply of power, lodging, entertainment, amusement, education, and telecom, excluding free services and employer-employee contracts.
4. **Defect (S.2(10))**: Any fault in quality, quantity, potency, purity, or standard of goods or services.
5. **Deficiency (S.2(11))**: Any fault, imperfection, or shortcoming in the manner of performance of a service.
6. **Unfair trade practice (S.2(47))**: Practices such as false representation, misleading advertisements, deceptive packaging, unsolicited goods, and hoarding.
7. **Restrictive trade practice (S.2(41))**: Practices that manipulate prices or affect the flow of supplies.

The Act also recognizes six consumer rights (S.2(9)):

1. **Right to safety** from hazardous goods and services.
2. **Right to information** about the quality, quantity, and price of goods and services.
3. **Right to choice** among competitive goods and services at competitive prices.
4. **Right to be heard** in consumer forums.
5. **Right to seek redress** against unfair practices.
6. **Right to consumer education**.

--- CENTRAL CONSUMER PROTECTION AUTHORITY (CCPA) S.10-27

The Central Consumer Protection Authority (CCPA) is established to regulate unfair trade practices and misleading advertisements. The CCPA is headed by a Chief Commissioner and has the following powers:

1. **Recall products** that are hazardous or defective.
2. **Discontinue practices** that are unfair or misleading.
3. **Impose penalties** on manufacturers and service providers for non-compliance.
4. **File class complaints** on behalf of consumers.

The CCPA also holds endorsers of misleading advertisements liable, including manufacturers and celebrity endorsers (S.21). The penalty for misleading advertisements can be up to ₹10 lakhs for the first offense and ₹50 lakhs for repeat offenses (S.89).

--- CONSUMER DISPUTES REDRESSAL COMMISSIONS (S.28-58)

The Consumer Protection Act, 2019, establishes a three-tier consumer dispute redressal mechanism:

1. **District Commission (S.28-40)**: Has jurisdiction over claims up to ₹1 crore. The Commission consists of a President and two members, one of whom must be a woman.
2. **State Commission (S.47-55)**: Has jurisdiction over claims between ₹1 crore and ₹10 crore. The Commission also has appellate jurisdiction over appeals from the District Commission.
3. **National Commission (S.58-73)**: Has jurisdiction over claims above ₹10 crore. The Commission also has appellate jurisdiction over appeals from the State Commission.

The following table summarizes the forum jurisdiction:

| Forum | Jurisdiction | Composition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| District Commission | Up to ₹1 crore | President + 2 members |
| State Commission | ₹1 crore to ₹10 crore | President + 2 members |
| National Commission | Above ₹10 crore | President + 4 members |

--- PRODUCT LIABILITY (S.82-87)

The Act introduces product liability provisions, which hold manufacturers, service providers, and sellers liable for defects or deficiencies in their products or services.

1. **Manufacturer liability (S.82)**: A manufacturer is liable if a product has a manufacturing defect, design defect, or does not conform to express warranty.
2. **Service provider liability (S.83)**: A service provider is liable for deficient service.
3. **Seller liability (S.84)**: A seller is liable if they alter a product or do not exercise reasonable care.
4. **Joint and several liability (S.85)**: Multiple parties can be held liable for the same defect or deficiency.
5. **Exceptions (S.86)**: The Act provides exceptions for misuse by the consumer, product used after expiry, and failure to follow instructions.
6. **Claimant's burden (S.87)**: The claimant must prove the defect, damage, and that the defect caused the damage.

--- E-COMMERCE RULES (Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules 2020)

The E-Commerce Rules require online platforms to make mandatory disclosures, including:

1. **Seller name and address**
2. **Import details**
3. **Customer care contact**
4. **Grievance Officer**: Appointed within India, respond to complaints within 48 hours, and resolve in 1 month.
5. **Fall-back liability**: Marketplace liable if does not provide seller information.

The Rules also prohibit:

1. **Fake reviews**
2. **Preferential listing without disclosure**
3. **Flash sales to corner goods**
4. **Discriminatory pricing based on user profiling**
5. **Pre-ticked checkboxes for additional charges**

--- PRACTICAL NOTES

The following are some practical notes for citizens:

1. **File complaints online**: Consumers can file complaints online via the e-daakhil portal (edaakhil.nic.in).
2. **Fees**: The fee for filing a complaint ranges from ₹100 to ₹2000, depending on the claim amount. BPL cardholders are exempt from paying fees.
3. **Limitation**: The limitation period for filing a complaint is 2 years from the cause of action. Delay can be condoned.
4. **Opposite party response**: The opposite party must respond within 30 days, extendable to 45 days.
5. **Relief**: The Commission can provide relief, including replacement, repair, refund, compensation, removal of deficiency, discontinuation of practice, punitive damages, and payment of costs.

--- CITIZEN Q&A

Here are some frequently asked questions by citizens:

1. **Q: What is the jurisdiction of the District Commission?**
A: The District Commission has jurisdiction over claims up to ₹1 crore.
2. **Q: Can I file a complaint online?**
A: Yes, you can file a complaint online via the e-daakhil portal (edaakhil.nic.in).
3. **Q: What is the limitation period for filing a complaint?**
A: The limitation period is 2 years from the cause of action. Delay can be condoned.
4. **Q: Can I appeal against the order of the District Commission?**
A: Yes, you can appeal against the order of the District Commission to the State Commission within 45 days.
5. **Q: What is the penalty for misleading advertisements?**
A: The penalty for misleading advertisements can be up to ₹10 lakhs for the first offense and ₹50 lakhs for repeat offenses.
6. **Q: Can I claim compensation for defective products?**
A: Yes, you can claim compensation for defective products under the product liability provisions of the Act.
